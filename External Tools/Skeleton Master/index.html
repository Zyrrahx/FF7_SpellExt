Skeleton Master FF7 battle animation slots

Add extra animations to a battle model, with no game files changed: load the model, pick animations, download the two new files.

1Load the model

Choose the model's two files together: the skeleton xxaa and the animations xxda (for Cloud: rtaa and rtda, from battle.lgp or the mod that supplies them). Use the files the game actually loads: a model mod (Ninostyle, for example) has its own.

2Load animations to add

Any xxda (another model's, or this one's own), a limit pack .a00, a single animation saved as .anim (one record, e.g. from the Retarget tab), or the .zip the Retarget tab's Download gives. The animation must be made for this model's skeleton (the same number of bones). The weapon track comes with it from an xxda (number + 34 in a party model's; 16 further on in a model of 16 bodies + 16 weapon tracks, an enemy with a weapon such as the final-duel Sephiroth mu: its header's two counts, read from the file), a limit pack (number + 8) and the .zip. A single .anim holds a body only: choose NAME.anim and NAME_weapon.anim together (or one after the other) and they pair by themselves; the Weapon column then names the weapon file. Copy names such as NAME(2).anim pair by the name without the number and the time each file was saved (a browser numbers each name on its own, so (2) need not go with _weapon(2)): one body and one weapon file of a name pair unless saved more than a minute apart; with several of a name, a weapon file pairs with the body saved within 2 seconds of it. When that cannot tell, the page says so and leaves them unpaired. Any weapon track can be chosen by hand in step 3.

The Weapon track of each added animation is what the game plays for the weapon while it plays (written at number + 34 on a party model; 16 further on, number + 10 in hex, on an enemy with a weapon: 16 bodies + 16 weapon tracks, such as mu; a model with no weapon tracks gets none). An animation that brings none (from a model without a weapon) gets the one chosen above: at the feet (the default) makes a track in which the weapon lies on the floor beside the feet for the whole animation, turning and moving with the model (side front lift place its grip: by default 250 to the model's left, 250 ahead, 30 above the floor; raise lift if the weapon sinks into the floor, lower it if it floats; Barret's gun-arm needs about 450 250 70, and Cloud's sword comes close to his legs when he steps to the side); held as in this model's idle keeps it in the hand as the idle (00 with its weapon track: 34, or 10 on a 16 + 16 model) holds it; empty writes the game's placeholder, which hides the weapon while it plays. A made track has exactly the body's frame count. KimeraCS fails showing an animation whose weapon track has fewer frames than it (the placeholder has none), at the numbers it lists (below 34; below 10 on a 16 + 16 model): the build says so when that would happen.

How it works

Why this was hard. A party model has 52 body animations: 0-2b its own, 2c-33 the limit pack's. The game finds the weapon track of body animation n at n+34 (hex), so the slots after the 52 are taken by weapon tracks, and a new animation cannot simply be added at the end. The free numbers are 68-9b and d0-ff; each new animation's weapon track goes 34 further on, and every slot in between is filled with the game's own placeholder. The skeleton file's header is updated so the game makes room for all of them. No DLL is needed.

Playing them. In the model's action scripts (the xxab file, edited in the Scripts tab), a byte from 00 to 8d plays that animation: numbers 68-8d are played by their own byte. Numbers above 8d are played with a7 NN. The table above shows the bytes for each new number. Never use 2c-33 (the limit pack) or 34-67 (weapon tracks) as body animations on a party model.

Models with no weapon tracks (the header's second count 0: most enemies, and any model that carries no weapon) have their new animations simply added after the last, up to ff, or put into an empty slot; the header's first count becomes the new total. A character's skeleton (type 2) is not offered 2c-33, since a party member plays its limit pack there.

An enemy with a weapon: a model whose header gives 16 bodies + 16 weapon tracks (19 vanilla enemies, and the final-duel Sephiroth mu with his sword) keeps body n's weapon track at n+10 (hex: 16 further on): the game draws an enemy's weapon from that number, and KimeraCS from the header's 16 + n, which is the same. Step 1 lists its empty bodies (0d-0f on mu, 0a-0f or 0e-0f on the others): fill those first; their weapon tracks are 1d-1f and so on, and both the game and KimeraCS show them. Beyond those, new bodies go in bands of 16: 20-2f, 40-4f, 60-6f, 80-8f, a0-af, c0-cf and e0-ef, each band followed by its 16 weapon tracks (30-3f .. f0-ff), so every body's track stays 16 further on; the gaps are filled with placeholders. The header keeps 16 bodies, and its second count becomes the rest. KimeraCS reads every record after the first 16 as a weapon track: it does not list the band bodies, and saving the model from KimeraCS turns them into 1-bone tracks (they are lost), as it does with a party model's 68-ff. Its animations also come with their own weapon tracks in step 2 and in the Retarget tab (where its sword's own movement is retargeted into the target's hand). A model of some other N bodies + M weapon tracks is not handled: the game's 16 further on and KimeraCS's N further on would disagree (no vanilla model is so).

A retargeted animation and its weapon. From the Retarget tab, "Send to Add slots" hands both over at once (for this page session only). Its Download gives one .zip holding NAME.anim (the body) and NAME_weapon.anim (the weapon track): choose the .zip in step 2, or unzip it and choose both .anim files together. Either way the Weapon column of step 2 must show the weapon file, and step 3's Weapon track must not say "none", before you build.

Testing. Put the two files (and a changed xxab) in a mod. If the log says "ANM ERROR" or the weapon is missing during a new animation, the files do not match the model the game loads. Keep the originals.

Other tools. Model editors that read the header's second count as "weapon animations" may not understand the new files; the game does. KimeraCS (read from its source, not tried) lists only the first 52 animations of a party model and draws each one's weapon from its weapon track at number + 34 frame by frame, without checking the track is long enough: a track shorter than its body there (the game's 1-frame placeholder above all) makes it fail showing that animation. The numbers 68-ff are not listed by it, so they never do; a body put into an old placeholder number below 2c needs a weapon track of its own length (at the feet or held, not empty). The same holds for an enemy with a weapon (16 + 16): it lists bodies below 10 (an empty body filled there needs a weapon track of its own length), not the bands from 20 on.

How retargeting works

What it does. Each frame of the source animation, every paired target bone takes the movement of its source bone: how far the source bone has turned away from the source's reference pose, the target bone turns away from the target's reference pose. With aim it is then pointed exactly where the source bone points; with reach it points from the model's origin to where the source bone ends. Bones without a pair keep their reference pose and ride on their parent, or swing behind it if they are loose. The model's position is the source's, scaled, and the feet keep the source's height above the floor.

Choosing pairs. Pair bones that do the same job: chest to chest, upper arm to upper arm. Limbs are usually aim; a pelvis that points the other way on one model is follow; a bone that only carries a shoulder out from the body is follow or reach. A target with a hand where the source has none can pair the hand with the source's forearm. Check the numbers on the hover label: bone numbers differ from model to model.

What only your eye can judge. The page measures nothing for you: hands through the body, feet sliding, a snap between frames or an odd twist must be looked for in the preview, and in the game. The preview draws the skeleton only (no model parts), so a pose that looks right as sticks can still show a gap or a crease on the model. Test in the game before you share an animation.

Where the results go. A retargeted animation is for the target's skeleton. Add it to the target model with the Add slots tab, or use the .anim with your own tools. Its weapon track goes with it: retargeted when the source has one and the map has a target weapon bone (the hand); otherwise, when the target has weapon tracks, made by the map's Weapon when missing (step 3: at the feet, the default; held as in the target's idle; or empty). "Send to Add slots" carries both, and Download's .zip holds both (with "empty", the .anim alone). Loaded back into Add slots, the step 2 Weapon column must name NAME_weapon.anim. A limit pack (.a00) goes in the magic folder under its own name, replacing the one it was made from: keep the original.

Internet. The 3D views use three.js, loaded from cdn.jsdelivr.net the first time (the browser keeps it afterwards). Without it the views stay empty; maps, retargeting and downloads still work, and bones can be paired by number.

5Save

It keeps the name you loaded. Only the changed scripts and their offsets differ from the file you loaded.

How the scripts work

What a script is. A list of bytes the game runs one after another. A byte from 00 to 8d plays that animation of the model; every other byte is a command, some followed by values (a word is two bytes, low byte first). a7 NN plays any animation up to ff. The game moves to the next byte only when the animation playing has reached its last frame. A script can be 256 bytes long at most.

How a script must end. ee: finished, back to the idle. f1: finished, holds the last frame (KO, victory). 9e: waits for the target to finish, then finished (the 2x-Cut turns). fe c0 and c1 loop for ever: right for an idle, but in an attack, a limit or a hurt reaction the battle waits for ever. The page warns about that, and refuses the crashes and freezes it knows of: a jump with no mark to land on, a command cut short, a script with no end, a move over 0 frames (a division by zero), a loop that plays nothing within one frame.

Saving. The file's other bytes never move: its header, scripts no slot uses, leftover bytes and an enemy's effect data stay where they are. A changed script is written over its old one when it fits and no other slot uses it; otherwise it is added at the end of the file and only its slot's offset changes. When several slots share a script you choose: this slot only, or all of them.

What is known, and what is not. Every command's length and the slot list were read from the game's code, and every script in the game's 481 xxab files reads cleanly; nothing here was seen in the game. Several commands' effects are not traced (they say so). For an enemy only slots 0-2 are known (idle, weak, dead): its other slots are chosen by its attack data. The preview shows animations only; test in the game before you share a file. Party slots 46-50 and 52-59 are the game's own scripts: the file's are never read.

New animations. An animation added with the Add slots tab is played by the bytes shown there (68 to 8d by their own byte, others by a7 NN). After building, "Use them in Scripts" hands the new files to this tab, so the numbers are checked and previewed.
